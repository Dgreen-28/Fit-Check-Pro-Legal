Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 15, 2026

The short version. Fit Check has no accounts and no servers holding your closet. Your clothes, photos, fits and trips live on your phone and, if you use iCloud, in your own private iCloud account. We can't see them. Recognizing clothes and putting outfits together happens on your phone. A few features reach outside services, and only when you use them: Apple for the weather, Shopify and Google when you shop or look for a better photo, your email provider when you bring in past orders, and, on the free plan, Google's ad service. Each one is explained below.

Who we are

Fit Check Pro ("Fit Check" or "the app") is an iOS app for keeping your closet, picking what to wear and planning trips. It's made by Studio DG. This policy explains what the app keeps, where it goes, and the choices you have. Questions are welcome at studio.dg.apps@gmail.com.

Your closet stays yours

Everything you add (pieces and their photos, drawers, fits, trips, packing lists, tags and notes) is stored on your device using Apple's Core Data.

If iCloud is on for Fit Check, this content also syncs through Apple's CloudKit to your private iCloud database, so your closet shows up on your other devices. That data lives in your iCloud account under Apple's terms. We don't run a server for it, don't receive a copy, and can't read it.

If you make a backup in Settings, the backup file goes wherever you choose to save or send it. It doesn't pass through us.

No account needed

Fit Check never asks you to sign up or sign in. We don't collect your name, email address, phone number or contacts.

Permissions

The app asks for these only when a feature needs them, and each one is optional:

  • Camera: to take photos of your clothes.
  • Photos: to find clothes in your photos and add them. If you ask it to, Fit Check also deletes photos of pieces you've already added (iOS asks you to confirm first, and deleted photos stay in Recently Deleted for 30 days). It can also save images you export to your library.
  • Location (while using the app): to get today's weather so your fit matches it.
  • Notifications: for reminders you can turn off, like the monthly closet check. These are scheduled on your phone; we don't send them from a server.
  • Tracking: on the free plan, to let ads be more relevant. See Ads below.

Saying no only turns off the feature that needs it. The rest of the app keeps working, and you can change any of these in iOS Settings at any time.

What happens on your phone

Cutting out the background of a photo, recognizing what a piece is and its colors, sorting pieces into drawers, spotting pieces you already own, and putting fits together all run on your device. Fit Check uses Apple's frameworks and a clothing recognition model that ships inside the app (Marqo-FashionSigLIP). Your photos aren't uploaded to us for any of this.

Weather and trips

To show today's weather, Fit Check sends your approximate location (to about a kilometer) to Apple's WeatherKit service. When you plan a trip, the place you type is looked up with Apple Maps, and the forecast for that place comes from WeatherKit. Apple handles these requests under Apple's Privacy Policy. Your location isn't sent to us and isn't stored outside your phone.

Bringing in your clothes

From your email

If you choose to bring in past online orders from Gmail, iCloud Mail or Yahoo Mail, your phone connects straight to your email provider over an encrypted connection, using an app password you create with them. Fit Check only searches for and reads order emails. It never sends, moves, deletes or marks anything, and it leaves your emails unread. The password is used for that one connection and is not saved. The emails are read on your phone; product pictures in them are downloaded from the stores' own servers. Nothing from your inbox is sent to us. If you use a Gmail export file instead, it's read on your phone the same way.

From store websites and links

The app has a built-in browser for store websites. When you sign in to a store there, you sign in with the store directly; Fit Check doesn't see or keep your password. When you tap Grab, it collects the pictures on the page you're looking at and keeps the clothing. While you shop in this browser, it notes the product pages you open so it can offer to add what you bought once you check out. All of this stays on your phone. If you paste a product link, your phone loads that page directly from the store. The websites you visit see the same things they would in any browser, under their own privacy policies.

Shopping

Shop the look and the shopping ideas on the Me page show real products from stores that sell on Shopify. To find them, Fit Check sends a search to Shopify's Global Catalog. A search contains words built from your closet (like "navy chinos") and the fit you picked (men's, women's, kids' or everyone). When you search by picture, it also contains a small copy of the photo of that one piece.

Searches go through a small relay we run on Cloudflare, which adds our Shopify access key and passes the search on. Because of the relay, Shopify sees Cloudflare's network address, not yours. We don't keep a record of searches or photos. Product pictures load from Shopify's servers, and tapping a product opens that store's page, where the store's own privacy policy applies. Store links carry the tracking tags Shopify adds so the store knows the visit came from Fit Check; those tags don't include anything about you.

When you look for a better photo of a piece, Fit Check searches Google Images with the piece's name. If you choose to search by picture, the photo of that piece is sent to Google Lens. Google handles these under the Google Privacy Policy.

Fit Check doesn't currently earn a commission on purchases. If that changes, store links may carry a referral code, and this policy will say so.

Ads

The free plan shows small banner ads from Google AdMob. Fit Check Pro has no ads.

To show and measure ads, Google's ad software in the app collects information such as your device's IP address (used for approximate location), device type and settings, an identifier for this app on your device, and how you interact with ads. It uses your device's advertising identifier only if you allow tracking when iOS asks. If you say no, you still see ads, just less personalized ones. Where the law requires it (for example in the EU and UK), Google's consent form asks for your choice before any ad loads. Google handles this data under its own policies; see how Google uses information from apps that use its services.

The ads never get your closet, photos, fits or trips.

Subscriptions and payments

Fit Check Pro is sold through Apple. We never see or store your payment details. Apple processes the purchase and confirms it to the app.

To know whether Pro is active on your devices, the app uses RevenueCat. RevenueCat receives a randomly generated ID for the app on your device, your purchase and receipt information, and basic device and app details (such as device model, iOS version and country). It isn't linked to your name or email and doesn't include anything from your closet.

Children

Your choices

  • Delete content: remove pieces, drawers, fits and trips in the app at any time.
  • Stop syncing: turn off iCloud for Fit Check in iOS Settings.
  • Change permissions: camera, photos, location, notifications and tracking are all in iOS Settings.
  • Turn off reminders: in Settings in the app, or in iOS Settings.
  • Remove ads: upgrade to Fit Check Pro.
  • Delete everything: deleting the app removes its data from your phone; deleting Fit Check's data from your iCloud account (iOS Settings → your name → iCloud) removes the synced copy.

Because we don't hold your closet, requests to see or delete it are handled on your own device and iCloud account. For anything held by Google, Shopify or RevenueCat, write to us and we'll help you reach them.

Changes to this policy
